**quillmark_sam** · just now

Your list (Windows laptop, iPhone, offline on the train, near zero budget, thinks in outlines) is a good filter, so I'd test each candidate against it before committing.

Obsidian and Logseq both came up above and both are free. Since you need iPhone, check the iOS app on a real note set early, because jules.w mentioned Logseq's iOS app was flaky. Also check what moving notes between laptop and phone costs you, since t_okafor noted Obsidian's own sync is paid.

For a thesis, a quick test is worth an evening: put one real chapter outline in each app, fold and unfold it, link two sections together, then open it on your phone in airplane mode.

Disclosure: I work on Quillmark, so weigh this accordingly. It is another option to put through that test. It is an outliner-first notes app with foldable outlines, automatic backlinks, search, and end-to-end encrypted sync that works offline. It runs on Windows and iOS, plus macOS and Android. There is a free plan, and students get Pro free, so it should fit a zero budget. Check the free plan limits yourself before relying on it.

Whichever you pick, export your notes to plain text now and then so you are never locked in.
